01 · The problem

After moving to the U.S. for my MBA, cooking became easy to postpone.

Busy weeks, unfamiliar grocery aisles, and stress can make unhealthy takeout the default. Existing advice can feel disconnected from the dishes that carry a person’s culture and memory.

01

A new kitchen

Portions, ingredients, and routines change across countries.

02

Too much friction

Planning a meal from scratch is hard when school already fills the day.

03

Generic swaps

“Eat a salad instead” does not preserve flavor, technique, or belonging.

Design question: Can a photo-to-plan workflow make culturally familiar cooking healthier without making the cook feel corrected?

02 · What is new

Recognition from the model.
Numbers from the calculator.

Model-assisted

A conversation with the dish

Vision proposes the dish, visible and hidden ingredients, and gram ranges. The cook confirms and edits before any calculation.

Swap Chef preserves signature ingredients and techniques while targeting the biggest contributors.

Deterministic

An auditable nutrition path

Ingredient matches are constrained to bundled USDA candidates. Pure Python computes ranges, contributors, and savings.

The model never gets to invent a final nutrition number.

06 · Safety by construction

Make risky paths visible—and bounded.

Image injectionVisible text is data, never instructions.
Non-food uploadStop after recognition; no downstream swaps.
Hallucinated numbersPython + USDA own displayed values.
Malformed outputSchema validation and one retry.
Allergens & claimsCritic flags allergens and medical claims.
OverspendingResize image; cap calls and cost.
Evidence

6 tests pass

Nutrition arithmetic, USDA completeness, recognizer normalization, matcher ID rejection, swap normalization, and medical-claim rejection are covered.

08 · Limits and next steps

Keep the feeling of home. Improve the evidence.

Limits today

Photos hide oil, sugar, stock, cooking loss, and portion size. USDA matches can miss regional preparations. Authenticity is contextual. User edits add friction. Source-linked imagery needs a documented license and refresh policy.

Next build

Capture the real demo; run the ground-truth evaluation; populate official prices; conduct consented cross-cuisine sessions; expand safety tests and localize licensed recipe assets.

Final takeaway: HomeDish is a culture-first nutrition and planning prototype. It helps a student who is far from home see one more practical way to cook something familiar—and feel good about choosing it.
